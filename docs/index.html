Frequently Asked Questions

FAQ: SCHUFA & Banking for Expats

  • SCHUFA is Germany's leading credit bureau. It stores bank accounts, credit cards, loans and payment behaviour. Almost every bank, landlord and many mobile providers check SCHUFA. For expats with no German credit history, "no SCHUFA record" means no approval from traditional banks. The first step is to use a no-SCHUFA bank to start building history.
  • With the right strategy, a solid score is achievable in 12–18 months: Month 1 – open an account with N26 or similar. Months 3–6 – apply for a secured credit card and pay it off in full every month. Months 6–12 – pay all bills (rent, utilities, phone) on time. Months 12–18 – request your SCHUFA report; DKB and ING become accessible.
  • For expats without SCHUFA, we recommend: N26 (VideoIdent, no SCHUFA score required, English app), Vivid Money (multi-account features, cashback, no SCHUFA score required) and Bunq (30-day trial, no Anmeldung needed to apply). N26 reports positive activity to SCHUFA and helps build a credit history; Vivid and Bunq do not report to SCHUFA.
  • Some banks allow you to open an account without Anmeldung: Bunq does not require Anmeldung to apply. N26 initially accepts foreign addresses. Most German banks (DKB, ING, Commerzbank) strictly require Anmeldung. Important: you must register your Anmeldung at the Bürgeramt within 14 days of moving in — it is legally required.
  • Under Article 15 GDPR, everyone is entitled to a free data copy once a year. Visit meineSCHUFA.de → select "Datenkopie nach Art. 15 DSGVO (kostenlos)". The report is delivered by post (approx. 1–4 weeks). Check every entry carefully for errors — even small inaccuracies can lower your score significantly. Errors can be disputed with SCHUFA in writing.
