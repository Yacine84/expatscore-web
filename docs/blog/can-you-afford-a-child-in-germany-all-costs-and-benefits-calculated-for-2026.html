Can you afford a child in Germany? All costs and benefits calculated for 2026

Calculating the costs and benefits of having a child in Germany as an expat, including financial support and tax benefits. Understanding the expenses and advantages to make an informed decision.

📅 May 11, 2026 ⏱ 3 min read ✍ ExpatScore Editorial
← Back to Blog

As an expat living in Germany, deciding whether to have a child can be a complex and personal decision, especially when considering the financial implications. With a range of costs and benefits to take into account, it's essential to understand the expenses and advantages of raising a child in Germany to make an informed decision. In this article, we'll break down the key costs and benefits to help you determine if you can afford a child in Germany in 2026.

Introduction to Child-Related Costs in Germany

In Germany, the costs of raising a child can be significant, but there are also various forms of financial support and tax benefits available to help offset these expenses. From childcare costs to education and healthcare, we'll explore the key areas where you'll need to budget for your child.

Childcare Costs in Germany

One of the most significant expenses for parents in Germany is childcare. The cost of daycare (Kita) can vary depending on the location, with cities like Berlin and Munich tend to be more expensive than smaller towns. On average, you can expect to pay between €200-€500 per month for daycare. However, some employers offer childcare benefits, and you may also be eligible for the Elterngeld (parental allowance), which can help cover some of these costs.

Types of Childcare in Germany

  • Kita (daycare): €200-€500 per month
  • Nanny or babysitter: €1,500-€2,500 per month
  • Parent-child groups: often free or low-cost

Education Costs in Germany

Education is generally free in Germany, including for expat children. However, you may need to pay for certain expenses like school materials, lunches, and extracurricular activities. Additionally, if you choose to send your child to a private school, you can expect to pay tuition fees, which can range from €5,000-€20,000 per year.

Education-Related Expenses

  • School materials: €100-€300 per year
  • Lunches: €50-€100 per month
  • Extracurricular activities: €50-€200 per month

Healthcare Costs in Germany

As an expat in Germany, you'll need to have health insurance to cover your child's medical expenses. The cost of health insurance can vary depending on your provider and the level of coverage you choose. On average, you can expect to pay around €200-€500 per month for family health insurance.

Health Insurance Options

  • Statutory health insurance (GKV): €200-€300 per month
  • Private health insurance (PKV): €300-€500 per month

Financial Support and Tax Benefits

In addition to the various costs associated with raising a child in Germany, there are also several forms of financial support and tax benefits available to help offset these expenses. These include the Kindergeld (child benefit), Elterngeld (parental allowance), and tax deductions for childcare costs.

Financial Support and Tax Benefits

  • Kindergeld: €219 per month per child
  • Elterngeld: up to €1,800 per month for 14 months
  • Tax deductions: up to €4,000 per year for childcare costs

Conclusion and Next Steps

Raising a child in Germany as an expat can be a significant financial undertaking, but with the right planning and understanding of the costs and benefits, you can make an informed decision. By considering the various expenses and forms of financial support available, you can determine whether you can afford a child in Germany in 2026.
