Changing careers when a loan is hanging over you

You did everything right: you studied, you moved to Germany, you took the job you could get. But the degree your student loan paid for led to work you do not want to do for the next thirty years — and the monthly repayment makes quitting feel impossible. Career changers exist in every country. What makes Germany different is how much of the change the state will pay for. Between the employment agency's education voucher, advancement funding and paid apprenticeships in shortage occupations, there are funded routes into a new profession that most expats never hear about — because nobody tells them, and because every programme has one unforgiving rule: the funding must be approved before you start. This guide walks through the three main routes, what each one covers, and how to sequence them around a student loan that does not stop just because your career does.

First, face the debt

Before you look at courses, look at the loan. Moving to Germany neither pauses nor erases a foreign student loan: you owe it in the original currency, on the original schedule, and the exchange rate can quietly make your repayments heavier or lighter over time. Three practical steps protect you while you retrain. First, contact your lender before you miss a payment — many offer income-driven repayment, temporary hardship reductions or payment pauses, but usually only if you ask in advance. Second, keep paying at least the agreed minimum during any training period and document every payment. Third, if your repayments are more than you can realistically carry, get help early: free, confidential debt counselling (Schuldnerberatung) is available from the Verbraucherzentralen (consumer advice centres) and charities such as Caritas, and counsellors can negotiate with creditors on your behalf. One German bonus: retraining and career-coaching costs you pay yourself are generally tax-deductible as Werbungskosten (work-related expenses) — see our guide to filing a German tax return for free for how to claim them. This article is general information, not financial advice — for your specific numbers, a free Schuldnerberatung session is the right next step.

Route 1: Umschulung with a Bildungsgutschein

An Umschulung is a full retraining programme that ends with a recognised chamber qualification (IHK or HWK) in a new occupation. Because it is built for adults who already have work experience, it typically takes about two years — shorter than a first apprenticeship. The funding instrument is the Bildungsgutschein (education voucher) under §81 SGB III. You can qualify if you are unemployed, or if you are concretely threatened by unemployment — for instance because your job, or your whole field, is disappearing. If approved, the voucher pays 100% of the course and exam fees at an AZAV-certified provider. Since January 2025, the Agentur für Arbeit handles the voucher even if you receive basic income support (Grundsicherung). While you train full-time, your unemployment benefit generally continues; basic-income recipients in qualification-oriented training receive an extra Weiterbildungsgeld of €150 per month, plus bonuses of up to €1,000 for passing interim and final exams. A minijob on the side — up to €603 per month in 2026 — is possible if it does not interfere with the training. The sequence is everything: book a counselling appointment at the Agentur für Arbeit, agree on the goal and the provider, get the voucher approved in writing — and only then enrol. A course you start on your own will not be paid for afterwards.

Route 2: Stay employed — employer funding and Aufstiegs-BAföG

If you have a job, your strongest move is usually not to resign. Under the Qualifizierungschancengesetz, employers can receive subsidies from the Agentur für Arbeit towards course costs and even wages while they retrain their own staff for new roles. The application runs through your employer, so the process starts with a conversation with your manager or HR — ideally with a concrete proposal for the qualification and how it helps the company. Quitting first destroys this route and most others, because several programmes are tied to an existing job or to a credible threat of job loss. For advancement qualifications — Meister, Fachwirt, technician or educator qualifications — there is a second pot: Aufstiegs-BAföG (AFBG). It contributes up to €15,000 towards course and exam fees, structured as a 50% grant plus an optional low-interest KfW loan for the other half. It is not income-tested for the course contribution and has no age limit; the course must comprise at least 400 hours. A separate maintenance contribution for full-time courses exists, but that part is income-tested.

Route 3: A paid Ausbildung in a shortage field

If no voucher is available and you need income from day one, consider starting a regular Ausbildung (apprenticeship) in a shortage occupation. Unlike a university course, an Ausbildung pays you: the training salary averages around €1,200 per month across occupations, with nursing at the higher end. If the salary is not enough to live on, trainees can top up with Berufsausbildungsbeihilfe (BAB) — up to €822 per month, needs-based and never repaid. The fields where funding, places and hiring are easiest are the ones Germany is shortest on: nursing and care, the health professions, IT, the skilled crafts (Handwerk) and early-years education. The payoff is real: qualified nurses (Pflegefachkräfte) earn a median of about €4,587 gross per month full-time, according to the Federal Employment Agency's Entgeltatlas. For someone servicing a student loan, a paid qualification with a shortage-field salary at the end is often a faster route out of debt than another degree.

What is not funded — and the recognition check to run first

One misconception costs expats real money: the Bildungsgutschein does not fund university degrees. Section 180(3) SGB III excludes degree programmes, so a master's or bachelor's cannot be paid for with a voucher, however employable it might make you. (Student BAföG is a separate system, with an age limit of 45.) Before you restart from zero at all, run one check first: can your existing qualification be recognised in Germany (Anerkennung)? For many regulated and academic professions, recognition — sometimes combined with a short adaptation or bridging course — gets you into your field in months rather than the two years a full Umschulung takes. The Agentur für Arbeit counselling appointment is the place to ask both questions: recognition first, retraining second.

A realistic 90-day action plan

  1. Days 1–14: Book a counselling appointment at the Agentur für Arbeit and prepare a one-page summary: your qualification, your work history, the occupation you want, and why your current path is ending.
  2. Days 1–30: Call your lender. Ask about income-driven or hardship options for the training period, and get any agreement in writing.
  3. Days 15–45: With the counsellor, choose the route — Bildungsgutschein, employer co-funding, Aufstiegs-BAföG or paid Ausbildung — and a certified provider. If your degree might be recognised, start the Anerkennung check in parallel.
  4. Days 30–60: Submit the funding application and wait for written approval. Do not enrol, sign or pay anything before it arrives.
  5. Days 60–90: Enrol, start the course, and set up a minimum-payment plan for the loan that your training budget can actually sustain.

Quick tip: The single most expensive mistake career changers make in Germany is starting a course first and asking about funding second. Every major programme — Bildungsgutschein, employer subsidies, Aufstiegs-BAföG — must be approved before the course begins.
