Anmeldeformular in English
The German Registration Form, Translated Field by Field

Every field of the official Anmeldeformular translated into plain English — what each box means, what to write in it, and the mistakes to avoid. Based on the official Berlin, Munich and Hamburg forms.

What this guide is: a line-by-line English translation of the official German Anmeldeformular — the paper form you fill in to register your address (Anmeldung) at the Bürgeramt. Every field in the form's own order, with what to write in each box. Based on the official forms of Berlin, Munich and Hamburg (linked below).

What is the Anmeldeformular?

The Anmeldeformular (also called the Meldeschein) is the paper form for the Anmeldung — Germany's mandatory address registration under §17 of the Federal Registration Act (Bundesmeldegesetz, BMG). You must register within 14 days of moving in, and the form is German-only in every city. There is no official English form for the whole country — Munich publishes an English version of its own form (linked below) to help you understand the fields, but you hand in the completed German form at the appointment.

This guide translates the form as it is actually printed. The base version is Berlin's form ("Anmeldung bei der Meldebehörde"), because Berlin is where most expats register first — and where the fields differ in Munich or Hamburg, the difference is noted in the table. The three cities' forms share the same core: your new address, your previous address, one block per person, your ID document details, and your signature. The differences are in the details — and the details are where people get stuck.

Bring the completed, signed form to your appointment together with your passport and the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung (landlord's move-in confirmation). The form alone is not enough — without the landlord's confirmation, the Bürgeramt cannot register you. For the full appointment process, see our Anmeldung guide.

How to use this guide

Work through the tables top to bottom — they follow the order the fields appear on the Berlin form. Leave official-use boxes blank: anything labelled "der Meldebehörde" (of the registration office), the date stamp (Tagesstempel), the municipality code (Gemeindekennzahl / Gemeindeschlüssel) and "Amtl. Vermerke" is filled in by the clerk, not by you. Dates are always written day – month – year (Munich instructions 2.1 and 2.9), never month-first.

Field by field: B — Each person (one block per person)

Each person being registered gets their own block. Munich and Hamburg fit four people on one form — if your household is larger, use another form. Spouses, life partners and family members with the same move-in date and the same old and new addresses fill in one form together (Munich instructions 1.2); one of them signing is enough. Children under 16 are registered by the person they live with.

Field (German)EnglishWhat to write
Familienname, ggf. DoktorgradFamily name (surname), doctoral degree if anyYour surname exactly as in your passport, including all parts of the name. ggf. means gegebenenfalls — "if applicable". For the doctorate, only the abbreviation Dr. is entered (Munich 2.7). A doctorate earned abroad can only be entered if you are entitled to use "Dr." in Germany (Munich 2.8) — do not write "PhD" or "MSc" here.
PassnamePassport nameYour name exactly as printed in your passport. Berlin asks for this separately from the family name, because the passport spelling is what the register uses. Berlin only.
Vornamen (Rufnamen unterstreichen)First names (underline your everyday name)All your first names as in your passport, in their official form. Underline the name people actually call you — your Rufname (the name you go by). Munich's English form renders this as "First name(s)" with the same underlining instruction.
Geburtsname / Frühere Namen (z. B. Geburtsname)Birth name / earlier names (e.g. birth name)The surname you were born with, if it differs from your current family name (for example after marriage). Leave blank if it is the same. Munich labels this "earlier names (e.g. birth name)".
GeschlechtSexUse the codes: M = männlich (male), W = weiblich (female), o.A. = ohne Angabe (not stated), D = divers (diverse) — Munich instructions 2.10. Hamburg prints männl. / weibl. as tick-boxes; Berlin leaves a blank to fill in.
Tag, Ort, Land der GeburtDate, place and country of birthDate (day – month – year), town and country of birth. Add the country if you were born abroad.
ReligionsgesellschaftReligious communitySee the Religion section below — this is the field that decides church tax. Only write a religion if you are actually a member of that church. Munich publishes codes for this field (see the code table); Hamburg offers tick-boxes; Berlin leaves a blank.
StaatsangehörigkeitenNationality(ies)Your nationality, in German — e.g. marokkanisch for Moroccan. If you hold more than one nationality, list all of them; stateless persons give their last nationality if applicable (Munich 2.13).
Ordens- / KünstlernameReligious order name / stage nameOnly if you have one — for example a nun's religious name or a performer's stage name. Almost everyone leaves this blank.
FamilienstandMarital statusSee the code table below: LD single, VH married, VW widowed, GS divorced, LP registered partnership, and others. Munich marks this field with an asterisk (see instructions) and publishes the codes; Hamburg prints the words as tick-boxes; Berlin prints the field with a reference "(1 oder 1 und 2)" whose meaning is not explained on the form — write your status in words (e.g. ledig) there.
Angaben zur Eheschließung / Lebenspartnerschaft (Datum, Ort, Land, AZ)Details of marriage / registered partnership (date, place, country, file no.)If you are married or in a registered partnership: the date and place (registry office, plus the country if it happened abroad) of the marriage or partnership. AZ is the Aktenzeichen (file/reference number) — fill it in if you know it. Hamburg splits this into separate lines: date and place of the marriage, date and place the partnership was established, and — for widowed people — the Sterbedatum (date of death) of the spouse or life partner.
Familienmitglied ist:Family member is:Berlin only — meaning not explained on the form. The Berlin form prints this next to each person block without further explanation. We could not verify what exactly it asks for. Leave it blank and ask the clerk at the Bürgeramt. (Listed here so you are not surprised by it.)

The person registering signs — or an authorised person, if someone else hands the form in for you. For that, the other person needs a signed Vollmacht (power of attorney) and their own ID: Munich prints the Vollmacht form together with the Meldeschein, and Berlin's signature line covers "a person with a Betreuungsvollmacht" (care power of attorney).

In Berlin and Munich the landlord's details go on the separate Wohnungsgeberbestätigung instead. Note: if you are subletting, your Wohnungsgeber is the main tenant, not the owner (Hamburg instructions ➀).

Religion — religious community (Munich instructions 2.14)

For registration purposes, only membership in a public-law religious society (öffentlich-rechtliche Religionsgesellschaft) matters. The codes most expats need:

CodeGermanEnglish
rkRömisch-katholischRoman Catholic
evEvangelischProtestant (Lutheran)
–keiner steuererhebenden Religionsgemeinschaft angehörendnot belonging to any tax-raising religious community
oakeiner öffentlich-rechtlichen Religionsgesellschaft angehörignot belonging to any public-law religious society

Munich's instructions list 20+ further codes for small communities (e.g. ak Old Catholic, lt Evangelical-Lutheran, isby Bavarian Jewish communities, jh Jewish Community of Hamburg). If you belong to one of them, write its full name. Islam has no code on the form — there is no church tax for Muslims in Germany, so most Muslim expats write – or leave the field blank.

Religion: the field that costs you money

The Religionsgesellschaft field is the most expensive box on the form. Germany collects church tax (Kirchensteuer) — 8–9% of your income tax — for members of the Catholic and Protestant churches, deducted automatically from your salary once you are registered as a member. The registration office takes the religion from this form and passes it to the tax office.

That means: only write a religion if you are actually a member of that church. If you write katholisch or evangelisch because it feels like the expected answer, you will be registered for church tax. If you belong to no church, write – (Munich's code for "no tax-raising community") or leave the field blank — the clerk will confirm it with you. Leaving the church later (Kirchenaustritt) is a separate official procedure with a fee that varies by state, so it is much easier to get this field right the first time.

Common mistakes

⚠ Mistakes that delay or invalidate your registration
Writing the appointment date as the move-in date
Tag des Einzugs is the day you moved into the flat (got the keys), not the day of your Bürgeramt appointment. The 14-day deadline counts from the move-in day (Munich instructions 2.1).
Ticking "Hauptwohnung" when you only have one flat
If you have only one flat in Germany, it is your alleinige Wohnung, not your Hauptwohnung (Munich instructions 2.2). Hauptwohnung only applies when you have more than one residence.
Writing dates month-first
German forms always use day – month – year. 03/04/2026 means 3 April, not March 4 (Munich instructions 2.1 and 2.9).
Forgetting to underline your everyday name
Rufnamen unterstreichen — underline the first name people actually call you. Without it, the office may use your first given name on all official mail.
Bringing the form but not the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung
The form alone is not enough. The landlord's move-in confirmation (§19 BMG) must be presented with the form — it is the single most common reason appointments fail.
Writing a religion when you are not a member
Writing katholisch or evangelisch registers you for church tax (8–9% of income tax). If you belong to no church, write – or leave the field blank.
Writing "PhD" or "MSc" as your doctorate
Only the abbreviation Dr. is entered in the register — and a foreign doctorate only if you are entitled to use "Dr." in Germany (Munich instructions 2.7–2.8).
One form per person — or one form per household?
One form per person is the rule, but spouses, life partners and family members with the same move-in date and addresses share one form (Munich instructions 1.2). More than four people on one form (Munich/Hamburg) — use another form.
Handing in an unsigned form
The form must be signed — by you, or by an authorised person with a signed Vollmacht (power of attorney) and their own ID.
Wrong previous address when moving from abroad
Berlin wants the country you moved from; Munich and Hamburg want your last address in Germany (if you had one) — Hamburg also wants the date you left Germany (Wegzugsdatum).

Frequently asked questions

Is the Anmeldeformular available in English?

There is no official English form for the whole country — the form is German-only everywhere. Munich publishes an official English translation of its form (linked above): use it to understand every field, but hand in the completed German form at the appointment. This guide covers the German form line by line so you can fill it in without speaking German.

Do I need one form per person?

Basically yes — but spouses, life partners and family members with the same move-in date and the same old and new addresses share one form, and one of them signing is enough (Munich instructions 1.2). Munich and Hamburg fit four people on one form; if your household is larger, use another form.

What do I write for Religion if I have none?

Munich's instructions use – (no tax-raising religious community) or oa (no public-law religious society); Hamburg offers the tick-boxes "ohne Angaben / keine". Only write a religion if you are actually a member — writing one registers you for church tax (8–9% of your income tax).

What is the Gemeindeschlüssel / Gemeindekennzahl?

The official municipality code of your city. Leave it blank — the office fills it in. Munich pre-prints its own code (09 1 62 000) on the form; Berlin's form has the same box under the name Gemeindekennzahl.

Can someone else hand in my form?

Yes, with a signed Vollmacht (power of attorney) and their own ID, plus your documents. Munich prints the Vollmacht form together with the Meldeschein; Berlin's form has a signature line for an authorised person.

What if my landlord will not give me the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung?

The landlord is legally required to provide it within two weeks of your move-in (§19 BMG) — refusing is an administrative offence with a fine of up to €1,000 for the landlord. See the Anmeldung guide for what to do step by step.
