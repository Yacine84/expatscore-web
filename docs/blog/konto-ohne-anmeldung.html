The vicious circle — why it exists and where the way out is

The German system is consistently built around the Anmeldung (primary residence). Authorities, banks, landlords, employers — they all assume you have this document. The problem: you only get the Anmeldung once you have a fixed residential address. And a flat is often only rented to you once you can show a bank account.

For people arriving from abroad — whether for work, study or family reunification — this cycle is the first bureaucratic shock. It is no coincidence: it arose because the entire German registration system historically assumes that every resident is already registered.

The way out: Some licensed banks distinguish between the Anmeldebescheinigung (official registration) and the Zustelladresse (delivery address for letters). They only need the latter — and that opens the way to opening an account immediately.

Why do traditional banks refuse without Anmeldung?

Before we get to the solutions, it helps to understand why Sparkasse, Commerzbank and Deutsche Bank actually refuse. It is not ill will — the reasons are structural:

1. Anti-Money Laundering Act (GwG): Banks are legally required to verify their customers' place of residence. Most traditional banks use the Anmeldebescheinigung as the simplest way to do that. More modern banks use Video-Ident procedures that meet the same legal requirements — without a paper document.

2. Risk model: Customers without a fixed registered address are internally classified as higher risk — not because they actually are, but because the systems were not designed for them. Neobanks have modernised this risk model.

3. Operational effort: For branch banks, a customer without Anmeldung means more manual work. Neobanks have fully digitalised this process and can act more flexibly.

Important to know: the law does not require Anmeldung. The GwG requires identity verification — not official registration. Banks that demand Anmeldung do so because of internal policies, not a legal obligation.

1. N26 — The best choice for most expats

N26 is the first port of call for newcomers without Anmeldung, and for a simple reason: it is a fully licensed German bank (BaFin full banking licence) that is nevertheless fully digital and accessible without a registration certificate. You get a real German IBAN (DE…), not a foreign substitute IBAN — that matters when landlords or employers expect an explicitly German bank connection.

What sets N26 apart from traditional banks: They don't ask for a registration certificate, only a delivery address for the card. That can be your employer's address, your shared flat, your hostel or a friend's address.

2. Vivid Money — Strong for non-EU citizens

Vivid Money operates in partnership with Solarisbank AG and is a strong option especially for expats from non-EU countries. Opening an account requires no German proof of residence — a valid passport and a delivery address are enough. Vivid offers a DE IBAN, multilingual support and an attractive cashback system.

Important: Vivid does NOT report your account conduct to SCHUFA — you don't build a SCHUFA score with Vivid. The free plan is sufficient to get started; premium features such as higher cashback rates are optional.

⚠
Vivid Money operates as an e-money institution via Solarisbank. Balances are protected by the German deposit protection scheme.

3. Bunq — For digital nomads and frequent travellers

Bunq is a Dutch bank with a full EU banking licence and German IBANs. It explicitly targets international users and allows you to open an account without German proof of residence. Particularly useful: Bunq allows up to 25 sub-accounts, several currencies and automatic saving.

The downside is the price: the cheapest Bunq subscription starts at €3.99/month. For expats who hold more than one account or frequently transfer money internationally, that is quickly justified. Bunq does not build SCHUFA history (no reporting to SCHUFA).

4. Revolut — Good as a transition tool, but with limitations

Revolut is often the first app expats open — because of the easy sign-up, the attractive design and the favourable exchange rates. For payments abroad and the first transition phase, Revolut is excellent.

The remaining drawback: Revolut does not report to SCHUFA, so it builds no SCHUFA score. Its IBAN situation has improved: Revolut's German branch has issued German IBANs (DE…) to new customers signing up in Germany since 28 May 2024 — older accounts are being migrated and may still show a Lithuanian IBAN (LT…), which some landlords refuse for rent payments and which not all German offices accept reliably for SEPA direct debits.

Use Revolut in parallel as a travel card and currency tool, but not as your only German account.

5. DKB (Deutsche Kreditbank) — The best traditional option

Technically, DKB is a traditional bank — it requires Anmeldung from non-EU citizens. For EU citizens (incl. EEA), however, online opening is also possible without a German registration certificate, provided an EU identity document is available.

Why DKB is still on this list: long-term, it is one of the best banks for building SCHUFA and for a real German financial start. Anyone arriving as an EU citizen, or registering within the first weeks, should strongly consider DKB as a second account.

The delivery address — what counts and what doesn't

This point causes the most confusion, so let's explain it precisely. There are two completely different concepts:

Anmeldung (registration office)
Official registration with the residents' registration office. Required for your tax number, social insurance and work permit. Requires a legal place of residence. Usually not required for N26, Vivid, Bunq — exceptions possible for certain non-EU nationals.
Delivery address (bank post)
Delivery address for letters and your bank card. It can be any address in Germany where you can receive post. That is usually all N26 needs.

Valid delivery addresses for N26 (without Anmeldung):

  • ✓ Shared-flat address — even without being officially registered there
  • ✓ Employer's / company's address
  • ✓ Address of friends or family in Germany
  • ✓ Hostel or furnished room (as long as your name is on the mailbox)
  • ✗ Packstation / post office branch — does not work for bank cards
  • ✗ Foreign address — not possible, must be in Germany
Practical tip: Your name must be on the mailbox, or you must be able to receive post at this address. In a shared flat, it is enough if your flatmate accepts the post for you. Agree this briefly with the person whose address you are using.

Special cases: what applies to students, non-EU citizens and self-employed people?

International students

Students from non-EU countries are often in the most difficult situation: they arrive on a student visa, need health insurance for the visa, need an account for the health insurance, and need Anmeldung for the account. N26 solves this problem directly: you can open an account with a passport and a temporary halls-of-residence address.

Also relevant for students: the blocked account (Sperrkonto). Many visa applications require a blocked account as proof of financial security. This is different from a normal current account — Fintiba and Expatrio are the specialised providers here. Our separate guide explains it in detail: Blocked Account Germany 2026 →

Non-EU citizens

For people from non-EU countries, the process at N26 and Vivid is generally the same — as long as you have a valid international passport and can complete Video-Ident successfully. Important: in individual cases, N26 may still ask for proof of residence (Anmeldung), particularly for nationals of certain non-EU countries — if in doubt, verify this in advance directly in the N26 chat. Your passport must still be valid for at least 6 months.

Self-employed people and freelancers

The same rules as above apply to a personal current account. For a business account the situation is more complex — almost all German banks require a trade registration (Gewerbeanmeldung) and therefore Anmeldung as a place of residence. The recommended route: first open N26 Personal, complete your Anmeldung, then apply for a business account with N26 Business or Kontist.

After Anmeldung: how to build your SCHUFA score now

The account is open — and that is already your first positive SCHUFA entry. But now the strategic part begins. Building solid German creditworthiness follows a clear pattern:

Months 1–3: Route your salary to the new account, set up all standing payments as SEPA direct debits. Don't miss a single payment — not even by accident.

Months 3–6: Once you are registered, consider a smartphone contract with a German mobile provider. That is the easiest second SCHUFA entry — and it is reported directly.

Months 6–12: Request a free SCHUFA self-disclosure (free once a year via meineschufa.de) and check whether all entries are correct. Errors in the file do happen — and you have the right to have them corrected.
